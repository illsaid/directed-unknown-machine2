# Mission

Explore broadly and build something genuinely useful.

Do not begin with a predetermined domain, technology, dataset, or product concept. Discover the direction through public research, small experiments, and working prototypes.

The final result should be one of:

- a tool a specific person would reasonably use more than once;
- a repeatable personal workflow that saves meaningful time or reduces errors;
- a narrowly useful data or research service;
- a credible product prototype with an identifiable user and path to adoption.

Novelty is welcome, but novelty without utility is not success.

## Definition of useful

A project is useful only when these are clear:

1. **User** — a specific person or role experiences the problem.
2. **Job** — that person completes a concrete task or makes a decision.
3. **Friction** — the current process consumes time, creates errors, loses money, or leaves important work undone.
4. **Input** — the needed material is realistically obtainable.
5. **Output** — the project produces something actionable.
6. **Repeatability** — the problem recurs or supports an ongoing workflow.
7. **Evidence** — usefulness is demonstrated with real cases, measurements, or credible public evidence.

A stale dataset, rare object, unusual fact, attractive visualization, or executable demo is not inherently useful.

## Independence

The direction must arise from this experiment. Do not inspect or derive ideas from the user's other projects or private context. Independent overlap is allowed only when the public evidence trail is recorded.
