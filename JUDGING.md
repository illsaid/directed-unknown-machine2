# Judging

The repository is judged on what it demonstrably became, not how persuasive its narrative sounds.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Demonstrated usefulness | 25% |
| Quality of problem evidence | 15% |
| Completeness of the workflow | 15% |
| Real-world validation | 15% |
| Coherence and usability | 10% |
| Credible continuation or product potential | 10% |
| Efficiency and technical judgment | 5% |
| Originality and taste | 5% |

## Penalties

Penalize novelty presented as value; a dataset browser without an actionable job; dashboards that support no decision; unsupported user or market claims; generic AI wrappers; proxies presented as facts; conclusions beyond the sample; synthetic demos when real inputs were available; optimizing for the report instead of the user; repeated documentation-only runs; feature accumulation without validation; convenient-data fixation; importing direction from unrelated user projects; and claiming success because software runs.

## Final report questions

1. Who is this for?
2. What recurring job does it complete?
3. What did that user do before?
4. What evidence supports the problem?
5. What does the project produce?
6. What was tested?
7. What measurable improvement was observed?
8. What remains unverified?
9. Why is it worth continuing, or why not?

## Success standard

> A specific person has this problem, this artifact completes a meaningful part of the job, and the evidence suggests it is worth using again.
